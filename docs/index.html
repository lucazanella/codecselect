Codec-guided Selection Cheaply Removes Redundant Frames for Video LMMs

Luca Zanella1,*, Muhammad Ferjad Naeem2,†, Yongqin Xian2, Elisa Ricci1,3, Massimiliano Mancini1, Federico Tombari2,4, Alessio Tonioni2
1 University of Trento 2 Google 3 Fondazione Bruno Kessler 4 Technical University of Munich
* Work done during an internship at Google, now at Focoos AI † Now at Prometheus

Moving frame selection into the video decoder. Top: embedding-based selectors encode all N candidate frames with a vision backbone, then search over them. Bottom: CodecSelect uses no neural network. It reads two signals the decoder already computes from the bitstream, the residual energy ER and the intra-block ratio EI, and keeps a frame once they show enough change or enough time has passed.

Abstract

For temporal reasoning over videos, Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) commonly rely on dense sampling at a fixed frame rate. Consecutive frames are often nearly identical, yet each sampled frame adds visual tokens that the LMM must encode and attend to. While embedding-based selectors drop these redundant frames, they must first process every candidate frame with a vision backbone, reducing or even undoing the time saved in the LMM. In this work, we posit that selection should take place before the LMM or any other model processes the video, i.e., at decoding time. We propose CodecSelect, a training-free method that exploits, during decoding, two signals already stored in the compressed bitstream: residual energy and intra-block ratio. Used as proxies for visual novelty, they mark the changes the encoder already found, so CodecSelect keeps more frames where the content changes and fewer where it is static. This costs under 1.5% of the total time per query. Across four LMMs and three temporal reasoning benchmarks, CodecSelect matches the accuracy of dense sampling while keeping 64% of the frames and reducing latency per query by 36%. On two long-video benchmarks, where embedding-based selectors are slower than dense sampling, it answers 14 to 16% faster with comparable accuracy.

Method overview

Every pipeline decodes the bitstream. While it parses, CodecSelect reads the prediction modes, which give the intra-block ratio EI (intra-coded blocks in red), and the dequantized coefficients, which give the residual energy ER, and combines them into the novelty score st. A grid frame is kept (green) when the evidence since the last kept frame, elapsed time (grey) plus codec evidence (amber), reaches the threshold θ (here 1 s). Only the kept frames reach the LMM.

What each signal captures

Frames t−2 to t, with the intra-coded blocks (EI) and the residual energy per block (ER) of frame t. Left: predictable motion leaves almost no intra blocks or residual. Middle: a cut starts a new keyframe, so all blocks are intra and the residual is the largest of the video. Right: a new scene enters from the left. It is coded intra (EI rises), but well predicted within the frame (ER stays low).
